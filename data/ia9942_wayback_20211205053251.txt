Import Alert # 99-42
Published Date: 11/24/2021
Type: DWPE
Import Alert Name:
Detention Without Physical Examination of Foods Due to Heavy Metal Contamination
Reason for Alert:
Heavy metals, such as arsenic, lead, cadmium, and mercury, are found in certain foods. At high levels, heavy metals can be toxic; thus, the FDA actively monitors the levels of heavy metals in the food supply and takes a systematic approach to reduce the risks posed by heavy metals, especially to vulnerable populations such as infants and children, who are most susceptible to some of the harmful neurological and developmental effects.
The FDA has set specific guidance levels for some heavy metals, such as arsenic and lead, in certain foods. However, the FDA generally has taken the approach of assessing on a case-by-case basis whether the level of heavy metals found in the food may pose a health hazard, such that the food is considered to be adulterated in that the levels may render the foods injurious to health.
This Import Alert covers specific firms that have offered foods with levels of heavy metals that may render a product injurious to health and that may be detained without physical examination in accordance with the guidance in FDA's Regulatory Procedures Manual (RPM) Chapter 9-8.
In making a determination as to whether a level of heavy metals in a food may render that product injurious to health, FDA takes into account the properties of the specific heavy metals present in the product, the amount of the heavy metals present in the product, consumption data available for that product, and the segment of population to be most at risk if they consume the product.
In 2019, there were multiple instances where sampling by FDA revealed high levels of lead in food products. The violative samples involved: two red mole paste products, a banana flour product, and a dried mallow product. The results found the products to have a high level of lead that may render the products injurious to health.
Guidance:
Divisions may detain without physical examination shipments of identified products from the firms on the Red List of this Alert.
Recommendations for additions to the Red List should be forwarded to the Division of Import Operations (DIO). DIO and FDA's Center for Food Safety and Applied Nutrition (CFSAN) will evaluate regulatory packages on a case-by-case basis.
FDA has established other Import Alerts for heavy metals in certain foods. This Import Alert is intended to address heavy metals and foods that are not already covered by a previously established Import Alert. If a product is covered by a previously established Import Alert, that Import Alert should be used, as noted below:
- Dried fruit containing lead falls under the scope of Import Alert 20-03.
- Fruit juices and fruit juice concentrates containing heavy metals fall under the scope of Import Alert 20-05.
- Spice and spice products containing lead fall under the scope of Import Alert 28-13.
- Candy containing lead falls under the scope of Import Alert 33-10.
If a previously established Import Alert does not cover all heavy metals found to be present in a product, then this Import Alert should be used in addition to the previously established Import Alert. For example, if a spice contains high levels of both lead and cadmium, the firm would be identified on Import Alert# 28-13 based on lead residues and this Import Alert for cadmium residues.
Release of Articles Subject to Detention without Physical Examination under This Import Alert:
In order to secure release of an individual shipment subject to detention without physical examination under this alert, private laboratory analysis of a representative sample of the shipment can be submitted to demonstrate that the product does not contain levels of heavy metals that may render it injurious to health.
Proper sampling and analytical records should be submitted to the appropriate FDA Division compliance office for consideration per the notice of detention.
Requests for Removal from DWPE for firms or products subject to this Import Alert:
In order to remove a firm/product from DWPE, information should be provided to the FDA to adequately demonstrate that the firm in question has resolved the conditions that gave rise to the appearance of the violation, so that the Agency will have confidence that future entries will be in compliance.
For guidance on removal from DWPE, refer to FDA's Regulatory Procedures Manual (RPM), Chapter 9-8, "Detention without Physical Examination (DWPE)."

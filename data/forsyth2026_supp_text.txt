531 Description of qualitative methods
532 Prior to fieldwork, we compiled publicly available information on the turmeric supply
533 chain to inform data collection. Where possible, interviewers were recruited locally from within
534 or adjacent to the communities under study, and were extensively trained in social science
535 research methods. Data collection instruments were designed as conceptual guides rather than
536 rigid scripts, allowing interviewers to probe organically rather than adhering to verbatim
537 question delivery. This method is in-line with semi-structured qualitative probing as described by
538 Patton (2015).
539
540 A central component of interviewer training emphasized reframing the research objective
541 away from individual attribution and toward the identification of broader industry practices. This
542 approach was intended to reduce social desirability bias and encourage candid discussion of
543 supply chain norms — recognizing that adulteration is a known phenomenon, and that the goal is
544 to understand its mechanisms rather than assign blame.
545
546 Given the complexity of color adulteration across multiple supply chain stages, iterative
547 probing over several months was used to establish precise and consistent terminology.
548 Triangulation was employed throughout, drawing on multiple inference types to examine the
549 same phenomena from different vantage points. This included interviews with diverse supply
550 chain actors as well as regulatory stakeholders. Convergent accounts across sources were further
551 verified against documentary evidence, including photographic documentation of adulteration
552 processes.
553
554 Patton, M. Q. (2015). Qualitative research & evaluation methods: Integrating theory and practice
555 (4th ed.). SAGE Publications.

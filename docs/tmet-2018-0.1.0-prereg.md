# Pre-registration: CFIA's open 2018-19 toxic metals file against every figure CFIA printed for it (tmet-2018 0.1.0)

PREREG-MARKER: tmet-2018-0.1.0-prereg

By MV2 of Munim, Inc. Prepared with AI agents working in his name. Text CC BY 4.0; code
Apache-2.0.

**Written before any result value in the file was summed, counted or tabulated.** The figures
below come from CFIA's report page "Toxic Metals in Selected Foods - April 1, 2018 to March 31,
2019" (inspection.canada.ca, date modified 2020-10-07; read 2026-10-30) and from the open
dataset's portal record (open.canada.ca dataset `2b47b561-44dc-4aeb-8a4d-72262f0dfe68`, resource
`0e040e20-f157-46ce-860b-186b294ce1f7`, size 3,221,954 bytes, portal MD5 `57017a0e…3705e18`;
Open Government Licence - Canada).

What was seen of the file before writing this, stated plainly: a structure preview of its first
rows, and a **names-only census** (sealed job `tmet-census-v1`, 2026-10-30) that counted rows,
samples, component, product and origin labels, units, plan codes and the *shape* of every result
cell (all 18,753 are plain numbers) without printing, summing or comparing any value. The census
facts used below are listed in their own section.

The grading code is `tmet_2018.py`, and its offline tests are `test_tmet_2018.py`. A sealed
compute job (`tmet-2018-v1`) grades this registration only after it is public on GitHub. The job
checks out the published code, then reads the file.

Nothing here is a finding about any product, brand or health risk. CFIA reports that Health
Canada found none of the levels a health concern. The values are total arsenic (not inorganic
arsenic) in the product as sold; powders (infant formula, protein powders, meal replacements) are
not comparable with limits set for foods as consumed.

## Why this is new

Searches on 2026-10-30 found the CFIA report page, news coverage of it (2025, protein powders),
and sites that repeat its printed figures. They found no analysis of the per-sample open file and
no check of the file against the printed tables.

## What the names-only census showed (before any value was read)

  row each; unit µg/g (= ppm) and plan `2018_TS013` on every row; MD5 and size match the portal.
- **Four sample IDs carry a "D" suffix** (`C2018TMET00039D`, `00113D`, `00183D`, `00191D`).
- **25 product labels.** Infant Formula - Dairy / Other / Soy add to **396** (Table 2: 395),
  with origin codes 0 / 387 / 9 (Table 1: 0 / 386 / 9). Protein Powder: **196** (195), origins
  72 / 34 / 90 (72 / 34 / 89). Meal Replacement: 198 with origins 16 / 135 / 47, exactly as
  printed.
- **Rice products vs bran products.** The 77 domestic and 2 unspecified rice products are exactly
  the label `Rice Products` (82 samples, 3 imported). The other 115 samples (labels such as Bran
  - Oat, Cereal - Corn, Cereal - Rice, Grain - Rice, Bran - Rice, Oil - Other, Popcorn, Crackers)
  must make up 99 bran products and the remaining 16 rice products, all 16 imported. The 11
  imported samples with "Rice" in their label (Bran - Rice 2, Cereal - Rice 6, Grain - Rice 3)
  leave **5** to find. Among the labels whose samples are all imported, only two whole-label
  pairs add to 5: **`Oil - Other` (4) + `Crackers` (1)**, or **`Popcorn` (3) + `Snack Bar` (2)**.
  Both reproduce Table 1 exactly for bran and rice products. These are readings R1 and R2.

## Rules (fixed now)

- **Columns** by header: `Sample No.`, `Product` (English), `Component` (English),
  `MaxOfAMOUNT`, `Report Unit`, `PLAN_CODE`; also `Function`, `Origin`, `Region`.
- **Value rule:** a number above 0 is detected; 0, a `<` with a number >= 0 or a not-detected
  token is not detected; anything else fails the gate. Each component's value is the largest of
  its rows. "Average" = the mean of the detected values (the report averages quantifiable
  results only).
- **Product types:** the three Infant Formula labels = infant formula; `Meal Replacement`;
  `Protein Powder`; `Rice Products`, `Bran - Rice`, `Cereal - Rice` and `Grain - Rice` = rice
  products; under **R1** also `Oil - Other` and `Crackers`, under **R2** also `Popcorn` and
  `Snack Bar`; every other label = bran products.
- **Samples:** all 987 are counted as the file gives them. The table rows are also reported
  (not graded) with each D-suffixed sample dropped when its base ID is also in the file.
- **"Matches"** means the value rounds to the printed figure at its printed precision (for
  example 0.0785-0.0795 for "0.079", 0.00125-0.00135 for "0.0013", 63.5-64.5 for "64").

## Gate (G)

File size 3,221,954 bytes (the portal's record; the MD5 is compared and reported, not gated);
the five key columns; 18,753 rows; 987 samples with IDs `C2018TMET#####` or the same with a D
suffix; each sample with exactly one row for each of the 19 components; unit µg/g and plan
`2018_TS013` on every row; only the 25 known product labels; every value passing the value
rule. If G fails, nothing else is graded.

## Predictions (P1-P6)

- **P1.** Infant formula (396 in the file): at least 10 of the 12 Table 2 cells match, and
  exactly 2 infant formula samples have mercury above 0.
- **P2.** Protein powders (196 in the file): at least 10 of the 12 cells match, and the file's
  highest mercury value rounds to 0.022 ppm and belongs only to Protein Powder samples.
- **P3.** The bran/rice split is **R1** (`Oil - Other` and `Crackers` count as rice products):
  R1 matches at least 20 of the 24 bran and rice cells, and strictly more of them than R2.
- **P4.** The file's two highest arsenic values round to 1.13 and 1.12 ppm, and both are
  `Bran - Rice` samples (the text's "brown rice bran and germ powder").
- **P5.** The text holds over all 987 samples: arsenic in 64%, mercury in 19%, one or more of the
  four in 70%, all four in 12%; cadmium not detected in 596-598 samples and lead in 656-658 (the
  printed counts plus at most the two extra samples).
- **P6.** The D suffix explains the extra samples: at least one D-suffixed sample is infant
  formula and at least one is a Protein Powder.

Competing readings, stated now: for P3, **R2** (`Popcorn` and `Snack Bar`), or a split CFIA made
sample by sample that no whole-label rule reproduces (then neither reading reaches 20 cells). For
P1, P2 and P6: the extra infant formula and protein powder may be ordinary samples CFIA left out
of its tables (or counted elsewhere), not D-suffixed repeats.

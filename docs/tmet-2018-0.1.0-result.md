# tmet-2018 0.1.0: CFIA's open 2018-19 toxic metals file against every figure CFIA printed for it — result

RESULT-MARKER: tmet-2018-0.1.0-result

By MV2 of Munim, Inc. Prepared with AI agents working in his name. Text CC BY 4.0; code Apache-2.0.

This grades the pre-registration [tmet-2018-0.1.0-prereg.md](tmet-2018-0.1.0-prereg.md), which was
public first, at tag `v0.4.83`. The sealed job `tmet-2018-v1` ran on 2026-10-30 at 15:57 UTC
(python:3.12, 1 GB, no host paths). It waited for the registration's marker to be public,
checked that its bundled `tmet_2018.py` and `test_tmet_2018.py` were byte-identical to the
published copies, ran the 13 offline tests and only then read the file (3,221,954 bytes; its MD5
matches the portal record; SHA-256 `5eedfe00…e332a8`). **The gate passed, and 5 of 8 graded
items held: G, C1, P1, P2 and P4.**

This is a reproducibility check of open government data, not a compliance finding and not about
any brand. CFIA reports that Health Canada found none of these levels a health concern. All
arsenic figures are total arsenic, not inorganic arsenic. Powders (infant formula, protein
powders, meal replacements) are measured as sold and are not comparable with limits set for
foods as consumed. It is not dietary or medical advice.

## Headlines

1. **Three of the five product rows of Table 2 reproduce.** Meal replacements match all 12
   printed cells (% positive, average of positives and maximum for arsenic, cadmium, lead and
   mercury), and infant formula matches all 12. Protein powders match 11: only mercury's share
   differs (99 of 196 = 50.5%, printed 50%).
2. **The file has two samples more than the report, and they are not the "D" samples (P6
   failed).** The file has 987 samples; the report counts 985. The extra ones are one infant
   formula (the file's origin codes give 387 imported where Table 1 prints 386) and one protein
   powder (90 unspecified against 89). All four D-suffixed IDs (`C2018TMET00039D`, `00113D`,
   `00183D`, `00191D`) are protein powders, and none of their base IDs is in the file. So the
   suffix does not mark a repeat, and it cannot explain the extra infant formula.
3. **The text's figures hold except one (P5 failed on mercury alone).** Over all 987 samples:
   arsenic in 627 (63.5%, printed 64%), one or more of the four metals in 687 (69.6%, printed 70%),
   all four in 120 (12.2%, printed 12%), cadmium not detected in 597 (printed 596 of 985) and lead
   not detected in 657 (printed 656). Mercury is in 193 samples, 19.55%, which rounds to 20%, not
   the printed 19%. With 985 samples it would fit only if at least one of the two left-out samples
   has mercury: 192 of 985 is 19.49%. That same assumption, if the left-out protein powder has
   mercury, also fixes the protein-powder share (98 of 195 = 50.3%). The file cannot say which
   sample CFIA left out.
4. **The printed maxima are where the text says (P4 and P2 held).** The two highest arsenic values,
   1.13 and 1.12 ppm, are both `Bran - Rice` samples imported from the United States
   (`C2018TMET00945`, `C2018TMET00934`). The highest mercury, 0.0219 ppm, is one protein powder
   (`C2018TMET00200`). Exactly 2 infant formula samples have mercury, as the text says.
5. **The bran/rice split is not a whole-label rule (P3 failed; the competing reading registered
   with it holds).** Only two pairs of whole product labels reproduce Table 1's origin counts for
   bran and rice products. Neither reproduces Table 2: R1 (`Oil - Other` + `Crackers` as rice
   products) matches 10 of the 24 bran and rice cells, R2 (`Popcorn` + `Snack Bar`) matches 11.
   The registration named this outcome as the alternative: "a split CFIA made sample by sample
   that no whole-label rule reproduces".

## Exploratory, after grading (not pre-registered)

Reading the published per-sample table after grading, we looked for a sample-level split. **One
split reproduces all 24 bran and rice cells and Table 1's origins.** Rice products are the 93
samples with "Rice" in their label, plus the four `Oil - Other` samples and one of the two
`Snack Bar` samples (`C2018TMET00977`, imported from Thailand, with 0.158 ppm arsenic, the
highest of the 104 bran-candidate samples without "Rice" in their label). Every other sample in those labels counts as a bran
product. Moving the Thai snack bar is what brings the bran row's arsenic maximum down to the
printed 0.079 (a granola bar, 0.0788 ppm).

The four `Oil - Other` samples are imported (two from India, one from Thailand, one unspecified).
The file does not say what oil they are. Rice bran oil would explain them being counted as rice
products, but that is a guess.

How unique it is: among the 10 samples of the four candidate labels (`Oil - Other`, `Crackers`,
`Popcorn`, `Snack Bar`), exactly 2 of the 252 ways to pick five reproduce all 24 cells. They differ
only in whether the fifth sample is the oil with no toxic metal detected (`C2018TMET00992`) or the
one cracker, which also has none. A sample with no detection changes no cell. We did not search
splits that take samples from other labels. This was found after the grade, so it is a lead, not a
result: a future check of the 2019-20 file could register it in advance.

## Limits

- "Detected" is the file's own coding (a value above 0). The file gives no reporting limit per
  sample.
- Which infant formula and which protein powder CFIA left out cannot be told from the file. The
  mercury arithmetic in headline 3 is consistent with a left-out protein powder that has mercury.
  It is not proof.
- The split in the exploratory section was found after grading by searching a small set of
  candidates. It is not the only possible split, and it does not show what CFIA did.
- The report's Table 3 prints identical infant formula arsenic and cadmium figures for 2017-18 and
  2018-19. No open 2017-18 file was found, so that cannot be checked here.

Prior art: searches on 2026-10-30 found the CFIA report page, news coverage of it and sites that
repeat its figures. They found no analysis of the per-sample file and no check of it against the
printed tables.

## Reproduce it

- `data/tmet_2018_samples.csv` is the sealed job's output, one row per sample: ID, base ID, D flag,
  product, type under R1 and R2, function, origin, region, number of the four toxic metals detected
  and all 19 element values in ppm.
- `data/tmet_2018_summary.json` is the sealed grader record: the gate, every check and prediction
  with observed values, the printed-figure arithmetic, census and provenance.
- `test_tmet_2018_result.py` is a pinned test. It does not import the grader. With its own code
  and its own copy of the printed figures, it rebuilds from the CSV and checks against the sealed
  record:
  - the counts, labels, D samples and Table 1 origins;
  - every Table 2 cell under R1 and R2;
  - the text's shares and non-detect counts, the arsenic and mercury maxima;
  - the exploratory split (24 of 24 cells) and that exactly 2 of the 252 candidate picks fit.

A sealed follow-up job (`tmet-2018-result-v1`) fetches these published files from GitHub and
re-runs the pinned test on them. The grading code `tmet_2018.py` and the pre-registration are at
tag `v0.4.83`.

This grades the value items of the pre-registration
[bpa-pah-report-0.1.0-prereg.md](bpa-pah-report-0.1.0-prereg.md) (tag `v0.4.86`), using the label
maps of the [census note](bpa-pah-report-0.1.0-census.md) (`v0.4.89`) and the grader of the
[grader note](bpa-pah-report-0.1.0-grader.md) (`v0.4.90`), all public first. The sealed job
`bpa-2018-2020-v1` ran on 2026-10-31 at 11:04 UTC (python:3.12, 1 GB, no host paths). It waited
for the three notes to be public, checked that its bundled `bpa_2018_2020.py` and
`test_bpa_2018_2020.py` were identical to the published copies, ran the 16 offline tests and only
then read the two files. Both MD5s match the portal records. **Both gates passed, and 4 of 8
graded items held: B5, B6, C2 and C3.** B2, B3 and B4 failed on the sample counts the census had
already ruled out, as the grader note said in advance. C4 failed on two of its six checks.

This is a reproducibility check of open government data, not a compliance finding and not about
any brand. CFIA's bisphenol reports note that Canada has no maximum levels for BPA, BADGE, BPF
or BPS in food; Health Canada assesses results case by case. Nothing here is dietary or medical advice. The files
report µg/g; all figures below are ppb (µg/kg), averages over positive samples only, as the
reports state.

## Headlines

1. **2018-19: the report uses two sample sets, and the file is the smaller one.** The open file
   holds 377 samples and reproduces Table 2 in every cell (coconut milk, infant formula, pie
   filling, tomato-based sauces, total). The summary's 381 samples and 43%, and Table 3's 138
   formulas (maximum 5.89, average 1.65) and 96 sauces, describe four more samples that the file
   does not contain.
2. **Those four samples are pinned down by the report's own arithmetic.** They are three infant
   formulas, exactly one of them BPA-positive at 5.89, and one sauce with no BPA. Adding just
   these to the file reproduces Table 3's formula row (138 / 18 / 0.92 / 5.89 / 1.65), its
   sauce row (96 / 48), and the summary's total (165 of 381 = 43.3%, printed 43%; average 17.77,
   printed 17.8). The pre-registration derived from the report alone that the 17 formula
   positives must sum to 23.72-23.90; in the file they sum to **23.81**. So the survey's highest
   infant-formula value, 5.89 ppb, is printed in the report but is not in the open data.
3. **2020-21 reproduces in full except two sentences.** Table 1's origins, all six rows of
   Table 2 and every cell of Tables 3-5 (BADGE, BPF, BPS) match. The vegetables paragraph's
   "163 (41%)" is the survey total; vegetables have 51 positives, as Table 2 says.
4. **Two co-occurrence sentences do not match the file (C4).**
   - "Of the 17 samples that contained BADGE, 8 also contained BPA, all of which were imported
     canned meat and fish samples." There are 8, but 2 are canned meats from Canada:
     `C2020BPHA00133` (Function `Domestic`) and `C2020BPHA00314` (Function `Unknown`, origin
     Canada).
   - "5 imported corned beef products tested positive for BPS as well as BPA." The five
     BPS-positive meats are all imported, but one (`C2020BPHA00130`, Brazil, BPS 0.96, the
     table's minimum) has no BPA.
   The other four checks hold: 14 of 17 BADGE positives are imported; the second-highest fish
   BADGE value is 51; all 31 BPA-positive fruit and pie-filling samples are imported.

## Exploratory, after grading (not pre-registered)

The 2018-19 sample numbers run in four product blocks: pie filling `00001`-`00100`, coconut milk
`00101`-`00150`, sauces `00151`-`00250` and infant formula `00251`-`00400`. Every sample sits in
its product's block. 23 of the 400 numbers are absent: 2 in pie filling, 1 in coconut milk, 5 in
sauces and 15 in formula. Three IDs carry a `D` suffix with no base ID (`00111D`, `00170D`,
`00171D`). The four samples the report counts beyond the file would be among the absent formula
and sauce numbers, but the file cannot say which.

## Limits

- "Positive" is the file's own coding (a value above 0). The files give no reporting limit per
  sample.
- The reconciliation in headlines 1-2 is arithmetic: it shows that four extra samples with those
  values make every printed figure agree, and that no other set of results for the extra
  formulas can (the 18th positive must be the 5.89 maximum). It cannot show why the open file
  lacks them.
- B2, B3 and B4 were registered before the census and name counts the census later ruled out.
  The grader note said in advance that they would fail on those counts; they are reported as
  failed.
- C4 is graded on the file's Function column. A sample whose Function is `Unknown` may still have
  been imported; one of the two non-imported BADGE-and-BPA meats is `Domestic`.

Prior art: searches on 2026-10-30 and 2026-10-31 found the CFIA report pages, the open dataset
records and news coverage of the 2023-24 survey. They found no analysis of these per-sample files
and no check of them against the printed figures.

## Reproduce it

- `data/bpa_2018_samples.csv` and `data/bpa_2020_samples.csv` are the sealed job's output, one
  row per sample: ID, product, type, Function, origin, region and the four bisphenols in ppb.
- `data/bpa_2018_2020_summary.json` is the sealed grader record: gates, every item with observed
  values, census and provenance.
- `test_bpa_2018_2020_result.py` is a pinned test. It does not import the grader. With its own
  code, its own label maps and its own copy of the printed figures, it checks against the sealed
  record:
  - every Table 2 cell of both reports;
  - the four-sample reconciliation and the 23.72-23.90 band;
  - the BADGE, BPF and BPS figures, Tables 3-5 and 2020-21 Table 1;
  - the two failing sentences, sample by sample;
  - the sample-number blocks and the absence of identical profiles.

A sealed follow-up job (`bpa-2018-2020-result-v1`) fetches these published files from GitHub and
re-runs the pinned test on them.
